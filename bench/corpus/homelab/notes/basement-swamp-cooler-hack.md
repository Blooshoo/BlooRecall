# The basement swamp cooler hack

Written 2025-09-14 (imported from the old notebook), refreshed 2026-05-03
at the start of its second season. Every spring someone asks why there is
a wet pad zip-tied to a box fan in front of the rack, so here is the whole
story in one place.

## What it is

An evaporative pre-cooler for the rack intake, household nickname **the
swamp cooler hack**. Construction, total cost about 30:

- One box fan (the one from the garage).
- One evaporative cooler pad, cut to size, zip-tied to the intake side.
- A drip tray made from the lid of a storage tote, with a garden-tap
  float valve offcut to keep the reservoir topped up.
- Hookup: the pad's top edge sits in a gutter made from half a length of
  rain downpipe, fed by a thin siphon line from a 10-liter bucket.

## Why it exists

The basement sits at 27–29 °C from May through September, and the rack
intake was eating that air directly. `bramble`'s drives ran 8–10 °C
hotter all summer, and the scrub schedule kept colliding with thermal
throttling. Central air does not reach the basement reliably. A proper
mini-split is on the someday list; this was the afternoon version.

## Results, honestly

- Intake air drops 4–6 °C on a dry day. On a muggy week it drops almost
  nothing, which is physics, not a defect.
- Drive temps in summer peak at 41 °C instead of 49 °C. That difference
  is the entire project.
- It is loud-ish. It runs only during afternoons of predicted 29 °+ days,
  controlled by a plug-in thermostat with a remote sensor zip-tied to the
  rack's intake.

## The catch (read before repeating this)

Evaporation means water, deliberately, near electronics. The rules that
make it safe: the tray is oversized and sits on a shelf BELOW the rack,
not above; the reservoir is outside the rack footprint; and the whole
thing gets a monthly look while running (in the job calendar as a
recurring "check the swamp" nudge).

The interaction with the basement's damp season is real — see
`notes/humidity-damp-rack.md`. The cooler does not run before May, and
the moisture gauge gets watched when it does. The two fixes fight each
other by nature; the calendar keeps them in separate seasons.
