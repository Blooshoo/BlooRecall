# The Pomegranate Incident

**Date of the event:** 2025-12-19, 07:40, before coffee
**Batch involved:** K-010, second stage, pomegranate
**Casualties:** one kitchen ceiling corner, one tea towel (retired with honours), zero bottles lost, dignity partially recovered

## What happened, precisely

December's K-010 first stage ran 8 days and came off dry. Into each 750 ml bottle went pomegranate seeds — a generous handful, maybe 80 g — plus, in a moment of Christmas-spirit sloppiness, two teaspoons of sugar per bottle, because "the first stage was dry". The bottles then sat on the radiator-side shelf, the warmest spot in the flat, because the counter was full of wrapping paper.

Five days later, running late, I opened the first bottle over the sink with a tea towel over the cap, because something had said "tub, tub" and I had said "later". The towel was not decoration. Foam climbed my forearms, cleared the tap, and decorated the window above the sink; the second bottle, opened with the respect earned by the first, performed the same trick in slow motion.

## The arithmetic afterwards

Fruit sugar + 2 tsp added sugar + a dry first stage with nothing left to eat + five warm days + pomegranate seeds that hold pockets of liquid = a bottle under real pressure. Nothing dangerous happened precisely because the towel-and-sink habit held; the incident is named for the mess, not the risk, and the risk was real enough to end the sloppiness on the spot.

## The rules that exist because of this

1. Fruit bottles get two days, three at most in winter; summer gets two. Calendar on the tub lid.
2. No added sugar in second-stage bottles unless the first stage ran past 10 days — and then half a teaspoon, not two.
3. Every bottle sits in the lidded plastic tub, always, since this morning. (It caught a gasket-leaker in June 2026; vindication logged there.)
4. Radiator-side shelf is now the ginger-bug shelf, not the bottle shelf. The bug enjoys the heat; bottles do not.
