# The Great Koji Flood

**Date of the event:** night of 2026-01-24, discovered 02:10
**Casualties:** 1.2 kg of inoculated rice (half recoverable), one tea towel, one bath towel, the kitchen floor's dignity, my calm
**Survivors:** the miso crock, untouched and smug next door

## What was being attempted

A January restocking run: a kilo of rice koji for the shio-koji jars (recipes/shio-koji.md) and a planned spring miso top-up. The incubation setup was the cooler box with two hot-water bottles and a soaked towel for humidity — soaked, which matters. The rice sat in one big aluminium tray on the upper rack, directly above a reservoir tray of water. The box is not tall. The tray sits close to the towel. You can see where this is going, but on the night I did not.

## What happened, precisely

Koji rice roughly increases in volume as it grows, and it grows most enthusiastically in the warm dark hours. At 02:10 the smell of something faintly sweet reached the bedroom — koji at full flood smells like a bakery with ambitions. In the kitchen, the swollen rice had heaved itself over the tray's lip in a slow beige waterfall onto the reservoir below, which then overflowed through the box's hinge gap onto the counter and, with the help of a bad tray angle, onto the floor. There was rice on the towel. There was towel on the rice. The hot-water bottles had slipped to the floor like they wanted no part of it.

## The cleanup, briefly

Everything wet went into the compost (the floor rice included — it had been on the floor and I am brave but not that brave). The recoverable half was still in the tray: it finished its run, smelled correct, and became January's shio-koji jar, which tasted exactly right and stands as the one clean escape of the whole affair. Scalded the tray, dried the box, scrubbed the floor twice because spore dust travels.

## The rules that exist because of this

1. Maximum 1 kg of dry rice per tray, and the tray gets a scrape-down at every turn — growth is a volume event, not a suggestion.
2. The humidity towel is damp, wrung out, never soaked. It is not a swamp.
3. The reservoir tray is smaller than the rice tray and sits BELOW the hinge gap. Geometry is a safety feature.
4. The box lives on a tray with a rim from now on. The counter has earned a moat.

Cross-references: the koji goes into recipes/shio-koji.md jars and one day a sibling for batch-M-002; the miso crock's own log records its total indifference to the chaos.
