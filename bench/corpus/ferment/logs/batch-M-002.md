# batch-M-002 — the big crock

**Packed:** 2025-10-19
**Crock:** 4.5 L water-seal crock
**Batch:** 800 g dry soybeans (1.72 kg cooked), 950 g rice koji (home-grown, October run), 330 g non-iodised salt
**Salt:** 12.5% of total weight — the full-red lane of recipes/miso-base.md
**Koji share:** ~36%
**Weight:** parchment seal, dusting of reserved salt, 1.2 kg weight bag

## Packing day

Beans simmered 4.5 hours to crush-between-fingers softness, mashed warm, cooled to 35 °C before the koji went in. Mixed with gloved hands, cooking liquid splashed in until the paste held as a ball without cracking. Packed in fistfuls with the corners punched down, smoothed, salt-dusted, sealed. The crock went to the pantry's interior shelf, out of the sun, next to the pickle jars like a sleeping bear.

## Check-ins

- **2025-11-19 (1 month):** tamari risen above the parchment, smell deep and sweet-savoury. Weight bag holding. Nothing to do.
- **2026-01-24:** same-day as the koji flood chaos next door in the kitchen; crock undisturbed, by choice and by fear.
- **2026-04-12 (6 months early look, smell only):** opened the crock lid for 20 seconds. The aroma stopped me mid-sentence — caramel, soy, something like good gravy. A miso behaving like a much older one.
- **2026-06-14 (young taste):** took a thin taste off the side as the base recipe allows from 3 months. Already complex: salty caramel front, long savoury finish, barely any bite. Six months in and it tastes like the 12-month mark of the ratio table. The pantry runs cool (18–19 °C), which must be pacing it gently.

## Decision

Leave it until autumn 2026 regardless of how ready it tastes. The young taste is a sample, not a harvest; the ratio ladder says 12 months at this salt and I am going to trust the ladder once, deliberately, to learn what patience buys.

## Notes

- The koji for this batch was the October 2025 run (the flood happened to the January restocking run, not this one — this crock was packed before I learned to weigh trays down).
- Surface has never grown anything strange; the tamari layer does the guarding. If that ever changes: troubleshooting first, compost second.
