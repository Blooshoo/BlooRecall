# batch-S-021 — the loaf where it clicked

**Date:** 2026-03-21 (mix 09:00, bake 2026-03-22)
**Recipe:** sourdough-country-loaf, temperature-managed
**Flours:** 450 g strong white + 50 g wholemeal, 500 g total
**Hydration:** 400 g water at 32 °C — 80%, warm-mixed to land the dough at 24.5 °C
**Levain:** 100 g at ripe peak (jar fed 1:4:4 the previous evening after three fridge weeks)
**Salt:** 10 g

## The bake

Water warmed to 32 °C so the finished dough sat at 24.5 °C despite the March kitchen. Bulk 4 h 30 m with four fold sets in the first two hours; ended at a measured 50% rise against the tape mark — the first time I stopped it on measurement rather than the clock. Preshape, 20-minute bench rest, tension shape, banneton, fridge 12 hours. Baked from cold: 240 °C covered 20, 220 °C uncovered 25, probed at 97 °C internal.

## Result

The first genuinely open crumb of the S series: irregular glossy alveoli from mid-loaf upward, custard-coloured interior, crust mahogany at the shoulder with proper blisters. Tasted sweeter than any winter loaf — the rye's toasted note up front, a long mild finish. Ate two slices standing up, warm, breaking the cooling rule again.

## Why it worked

Everything S-014 got wrong, this got right, and the only real variable was temperature: 24.5 °C dough instead of 17.5, 80% hydration behaving itself because the dough was actually fermenting. The 1:4:4 wake-up feed after the fridge weeks gave the levain its legs back.

## Carry-forward

The temperature targets and the 50% tape rule went straight into guides/sourdough-complete-guide.md. The next three loaves (S-022, S-023, S-024) held the same channel within a degree, which is how a fluke becomes a method.
