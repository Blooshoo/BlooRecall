# batch-K-049 — the peach bottles

**Cycle:** brewed 2026-07-12, bottled 2026-07-19, fridge 2026-07-21
**Vessel:** 3.8 L clip-lid jar (resting lid), coolest shelf, kitchen 24–26 °C
**Brew:** 3.8 L water, 190 g white sugar (50 g/L), 6 plain black bags
**Starter:** 380 ml (10%) from K-048 plus pancake
**Harvest:** day 7, on smell — bright, dry, tea still present

## Bottling

Nine 750 ml flip-tops, all in the tub. Six got peaches: three thin slices per bottle, no added sugar (the first stage ran only 7 days and kept a whisper of sweetness). Two plain, one ginger control. The peach bottles sat their second stage 46 hours at 22 °C, then into the fridge — one of them, bottle 4, rode an extra 6 hours to see the difference.

## Tasting

- 46-hour bottles, day 1 in the fridge: stone-fruit aroma loud on opening, moderate sparkle, sweet-tart balance right where I wanted it. The summer peak of the whole K series.
- The 52-hour bottle (number 4): noticeably more aggressive at the pop, fruit aroma slightly stripped. Six hours cost more than it bought.
- Day 3 in the fridge: the 46-hour bottles have lost their sweetness and gained dryness — the two-day window is real.

## Lessons

- Peach wants 46 hours at 22 °C, full stop; longer is a different drink.
- Summer first stages of 7 days leave enough residual sugar that the second stage needs none added.
- The tub caught nothing this time, but bottle 4's enthusiasm justified its existence again.
