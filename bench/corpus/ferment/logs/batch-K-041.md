# batch-K-041

**Cycle:** 2026-05-12 to 2026-05-24
**Vessel:** 3.2 L wide jar on the seedling mat, dial at 26.5 °C
**Brew:** 1.5 L of the batch in the small vessel for a side trial: 60 g/L sugar, black tea only
**Starter:** 10% from K-040 plus pancake
**Harvest:** day 12

## The experiment

The mat dial got knocked to 26.5 °C during a cleaning shuffle and I left it there to see what a full cycle at the warm edge would do. Answer, by day 6: everything. The culture layer formed in three days flat, the surface smell turned bright by day 5, and by day 8 the liquid was already drier than K-040 at day 10. I let it run to day 12 out of curiosity and to map where "too far" actually sits.

## Result

The sharpest liquid this kitchen has produced — nearly cider, genuinely sour first, faint tea behind. Not drinkable neat for either of us; excellent as a 10% starter cut for the next batch (which is exactly where most of it went), and one bottle kept back unflavoured for salad dressing experiments.

## Lessons

- The mat at 26.5 °C compresses a 9-day cycle into about 7; at day 10+ at this temperature you are making vinegar on purpose.
- Heat speed does not buy flavour: the K-040 control at 24 °C tasted rounder at its own harvest.
- The "sharp bottle for dressing" is now a permanent minor product — every fourth cycle, one bottle runs long on purpose.

Method context: guides/kombucha-year.md; the deliberate long-run bottle is the V-series' little sibling.
