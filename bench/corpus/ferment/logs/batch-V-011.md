# batch-V-011 — red wine vinegar

**Started:** 2026-01-05
**Vessel:** 5 L wide-mouth jar, cloth cover
**Input:** 1.5 L leftover red wine (party remains, two half-bottles and a full one), 300 ml raw finished liquid from V-008 (the apple-scrap series) as starter, 500 ml water to drop the alcohol into the comfortable range
**Room:** pantry shelf, 20–24 °C

## Log

- **2026-01-05:** everything into the jar, cloth on, stir once. Wine smell with a sour edge by day 4 — the culture waking.
- **2026-01-16:** first felt mat formed across the top, smooth and leathery. Left in place per triage (troubleshooting/vinegar-triage.md); it is the culture's own work.
- **2026-02-20:** first taste: harsh, still wine-forward, short finish. Expected; two months is nothing.
- **2026-04-12:** racking #1 off the sediment, taste noticeably rounder. The strands drifting in the liquid are normal and get strained at bottling.
- **2026-06-01:** racking #2. Losing the raw alcohol note entirely.
- **2026-07-05:** titration strip reads 3.2% acidity. Tastes rounded, cider-adjacent, soft tannic tail from the wine. Bottled two 250 ml bottles dark for the pantry; the rest stays in the jar to keep deepening.

## Lessons

- Wine at drinking strength wants diluting here; the undiluted half-bottle trial (V-010) crawled for months.
- The felt mat doubled in size by April; lifting it out with a slotted spoon before any tasting keeps the jar clean.
- Party leftovers are a legitimate vinegar pipeline. This jar cost nothing but patience and produced the best condiment in the kitchen.
