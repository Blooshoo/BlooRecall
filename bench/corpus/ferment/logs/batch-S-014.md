# batch-S-014 — the cold-kitchen loaf

**Date:** 2025-12-07 (mix 08:30, bake next morning)
**Recipe:** sourdough-country-loaf, winter adjustment
**Flours:** 400 g strong white + 100 g dark rye (20%), 500 g total
**Hydration:** 390 g water — 78%, an experiment that winter did not ask for
**Levain:** 100 g, built 1:2:2 the night before
**Salt:** 10 g

## The bake

Kitchen sat at 17 °C all day; I mixed with cold tap water because the recipe said nothing about water temperature and I did not yet know better. Autolyse 1 h, levain in, salt in, four fold sets over two hours, then a 5-hour bulk that produced almost no visible rise. Shaped anyway, retarded 11 hours at 4 °C, baked from cold: 235 °C covered 20 minutes, 220 °C uncovered 22.

## Result

Dense, grey-tinged crumb, tight and even, crust pale with few blisters. Edible, good toasted, completely wrong as an open-crumb loaf. Tasted faintly of nothing in particular — under-fermented dough has no flavour story to tell.

## Diagnosis

Under-fermented, cold all the way through. The 78% hydration made the slack dough even harder to read; cold dough at 17 °C simply never built gas. The strip thermometer arrived the following week and immediately showed the mixing bowl at 17.5 °C while the recipe world apparently lived at 24.

## What changed because of this loaf

Warm-water mixing to target dough temperature, the light-on oven as a bulk box, and the eventual guide section on this exact failure (guides/sourdough-complete-guide.md, the tight grey crumb entry). S-021 is the redemption loaf; this one is the receipt.
