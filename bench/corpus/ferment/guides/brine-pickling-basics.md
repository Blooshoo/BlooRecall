# Brine pickling basics

Written 2025-10-19, back when every jar was still a surprise. The percentages and habits that everything else on this wiki assumes.

## The two percentages I actually use

- 2.0–2.2% salt by weight of vegetables (plus just enough water to cover) — krauts and kimchi. Crunchy, sour, weeks not months.
- 3.5% brine by weight of WATER (35 g per litre) — pickles and chilies standing whole in liquid. Firmer texture, slower sour, months of fridge life.

Everything else is decoration. Weigh the salt, every time; spoons lie, and two brands of coarse salt differ by 30% in a teaspoon.

## Water and salt

- Chlorinated tap water slowed my first cucumber batch visibly — bubbles stopped for two days. Rest tap water an hour, or use filtered.
- Non-iodised salt only. Iodine and anti-caking agents both make the brine dull and occasionally the surface cloudy-grey — see guides/milky-brine.md for what normal milky actually looks like.

## The routine

1. Pack vegetables tight, leave 3 cm headspace.
2. Pour brine over, weigh everything down so nothing pokes above the surface. A cabbage leaf under the weight catches strays.
3. Lid loose or cloth. Room out of sun.
4. Taste from day 5. Cucumbers are done in 7–10 days at 20 °C; chilies want 14; kraut 10.
5. Fridge when it tastes right. It keeps souring slowly forever after, which is how day-40 kraut happens.

## Reading the jar

Bubbles rising through the brine for the first week: correct. Milky brine: correct (guide above). A smooth tan sheet on top: skim it. Anything fuzzy: see guides/surface-fuzz-and-film.md and act accordingly.
