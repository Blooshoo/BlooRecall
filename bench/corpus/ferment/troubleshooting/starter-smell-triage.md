# Starter smell triage

Written 2026-01-11, after a rough fortnight with the rye starter during the cold snap. Smell is the fastest instrument in the kitchen: the nose names the problem before the jar visibly does anything. Everything below is keyed to what the open jar actually smells like, drawn from a year of S-series misadventures.

## The paint-thinner family

A sharp, solventy edge — closest cousin is nail-polish remover — means one thing in this kitchen: the culture has run out of food and started consuming itself. Every time it has happened here, the cause was the same (too long between feedings, usually cold-room related) and the fix was boring: feed 1:2:2 (30 g starter : 60 g water : 60 g flour) twice a day and keep the jar on the warm shelf above the kettle. It rebalanced in about two days, every time. If the jar is also runny and separating into layers, hold back 10 g of water on the next feed. Full recovery walkthrough lives in guides/starter-rescue.md.

## The dairy-gone-bad family

A heavier, sweeter note, like milk left out too long, shows up when the jar sits warm and underfed together. One wholemeal feed, a cooler spot, then back to normal; a day usually settles it. This one followed a heatwave window in the S-015 week.

## The nothing-family

Clean nose, no sharpness, but also no aroma at all: a tired jar, usually one that has lived on white flour alone too long. One feed with dark rye wakes the aroma up within a cycle. Not a problem, just a nudge.

## Clean nose but no lift

When it smells right and will not rise, the culture is fine and the flour or the temperature is not. Winter answer: warm the mixing water to 30 °C and move the jar; flour answer: switch one daily feed to whole rye for three days. Both fixes are documented with numbers in troubleshooting/slow-starter-winter.md.

## When to give up

Pink or orange tint anywhere, or a putrid note that survives two good feeds. Neither has happened here yet — the January 2026 cold snap was the worst it has behaved, and even that recovered — but the rule stands in writing so future-me does not negotiate. If the day comes: bin the jar, and start again from scratch; the September 2025 jar took five days from nothing, and it was not the end of the world, only of the jar.
