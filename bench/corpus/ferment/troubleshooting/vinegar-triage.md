# Vinegar triage

2026-04-12, assembled from the V series: the apple-scrap run (V-001 through V-008) and the red wine jar (V-011). Vinegar is the most patient ferment in this kitchen and the easiest to misread; this page is the decoder.

## The grey mat on the surface

A smooth, leathery layer forming across the top of a working batch is the culture building its own house. Leave it in place while the batch runs; lift it out with a slotted spoon before any bottling or tasting; and do not use it to kick-start the next batch — the finished liquid itself carries everything needed. V-011's layer arrived on day 11 and had doubled by April; the batch under it tested beautifully either way. If it dries out at the edges and turns papery, the room is too airy — nudge the cloth tighter, not closed.

## Webs hanging in the liquid

Fine strands that drift like smoke when the jar or bottle swirls are normal in an actively working batch, especially in the first month. They are the culture's own scaffolding. Strain through cloth at bottling, and stop staring at them — a month of V-011 was inspected hourly for no reason.

## It stays wine, never turns

Three causes, all personal experience: the cloth got swapped for a lid (no air, and this process breathes), the room sat under 18 °C all February (the jar sulked on the pantry's cold shelf), or the starting alcohol was too weak for the culture to bother with (V-010's diluted experiment crawled until it was strengthened). Fixes, in order: cloth not lid, a warmer shelf, and a starting liquid that actually has something to convert.

## It went sharp past vinegar

If a batch turns to nail-polish solvent instead of clean sour, it went over to the wrong workers entirely — V-003, the apple-scrap jar I forgot for a season, did exactly this. There is no pulling it back; compost the batch and scald the vessel. The consolation, discovered that week: the K-044 over-harvested kombucha jar became the family's best vinegar starter, so the lesson generalises — sharp is a fork, not a wall.

## Sediment and colour

A dark layer of sediment at the bottom after a couple of months is spent cells and fruit solids; rack off the clear liquid above it. Colour deepening is normal. Cloudy is normal. The jar is doing chemistry, not theatre.
