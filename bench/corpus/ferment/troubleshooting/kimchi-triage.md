# Kimchi triage

2026-05-17. Written after KC-002 managed to fail in two directions at once, and after realising that every kimchi problem here had already happened at least twice. Diagnose by the section heading, act by the paragraph.

## Nothing is happening in the jar

Day three and the surface is still as a snow globe: no lift under the lid, no tang in the nose, no bubbles in the brine. All three of my failures trace to the same cause — the batch spent its first days somewhere under 18 °C, once on the stone windowsill in February, once in the spring cold snap. Moving the container next to the kettle (about 22 °C) woke it inside a day, every time. Warmth first, patience second, compost third. Dry-salted batches at 2.2% are slightly slower to start than the old soaked ones; give warmth a full 24 hours before despairing.

## The cabbage went soft by day five

Crunch gone, everything slippery, brine gone stringy. Mine did exactly this at 3% salt with the batch sitting above the radiator: too much salt stresses the culture and warmth hands the job to the wrong workers. Since moving to 2.2% by weight (recipes/kimchi-baechu-v2.md) and keeping the first week under 21 °C, every batch has kept its crunch through three weeks in the fridge.

## The garlic went blue-green

Harmless. A reaction between garlic compounds and the developing acid, most common with fresh-harvest garlic — KC-003 turned half its cloves ghostly blue by day two and tasted identical to its white-clove siblings. Looks like a crime scene, is a chemistry lesson.

## Too sour by day three

A warm spot, or leftover brine from a previous batch in the jar kicking things off at speed. Not a fault exactly, but if you wanted slow: move the container to the fridge earlier and use cleaned jars. Sour kimchi is still perfectly good cooked into pancakes, which is the traditional safety net and a genuinely delicious one.

## White dust on the jar or milky liquid

Both normal. The dust is salt minerals drying on the glass; the milkiness is the culture working. The full tour of what the jar legitimately looks like lives in guides/milky-brine.md, and the fuzz-versus-film decision tree lives in guides/surface-fuzz-and-film.md.
