# Kombucha with no tingle

Written 2026-08-09, after two August bottles poured dead — still as tap water, no prickle on the tongue, no foam, no anything. Same batch as the lively neighbours, same kitchen, same fruit. Here is every cause of still bottles this kitchen has identified, ranked by how often each was the culprit across the K series.

## The room was too cool

The second stage makes both flavour and gas, and under 20 °C it does neither with any hurry. The August cold snap dropped evening temperatures to 19 °C and the bottles sat through it. Fix that worked: the bottle tub moved next to the kettle, 24–26 °C for 48 hours, and the difference at opening was night and day. In winter this is simply the standing arrangement; in August it was a surprise.

## Not enough sugar left to eat

If the first stage runs long, the liquid is already dry when it goes into the bottle and the culture has nothing left to work with — the K-048 holiday batch went 14 days and produced three still bottles from its fruit runs. Fix: half a teaspoon of sugar per 500 ml bottle at bottling time, and only then. More than that and you re-enact the pomegranate incident (logs/2025-12-the-pomegranate-incident.md), which is the opposite failure with the same ingredient.

## The seal

Two of the twelve flip-tops fail the monthly water test — gaskets gone hard, gas leaking out silently over days. The tell is a bottle that hisses weakly and tastes perfectly good but utterly unmoving. Fix: water-test every bottle once a month (fill, seal, invert, watch the rim); failures go on storage duty, permanently. The two hard-gasket bottles are now pen pals with the flour.

## Opened too early

Under 36 hours at cool room temperature, most bottles here are simply not ready, and impatience reads as a dead bottle. The calendar matters less than the warmth — 46 hours at 22 °C beats 60 hours at 18 °C — but the floor is the floor. Warmth first, patience second.

## Fridge too soon

Refrigeration pauses the work entirely; it is the brake, and it belongs at the taste peak, not as a first resort. The fix is embarrassing in its simplicity: bring the bottle back out to the warm spot for a day and try again. Only once (one of the K-045 bottles, chilled on day one by an over-eager housemate) did a bottle need two warm days.

## The one-line summary

Still bottles are almost always temperature, sugar, or seals, in that order — in that order every single time here — and all three are fixed before bottling day, not after.
