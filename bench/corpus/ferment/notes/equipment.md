# Equipment

Updated 2026-04-05, after a year of learning what actually gets used.

## Jars and vessels

- Two 750 ml and four 1 L straight-sided glass jars with screw lids — daily drivers for brine work.
- One 2 L and one 3.2 L wide jar for krauts; the 3.2 L takes a 1 kg cabbage batch exactly.
- Twelve 750 ml flip-top bottles for kombucha. Two have hardened gaskets and failed the water test in August — they are on storage duty now (see troubleshooting/kombucha-no-tingle.md).
- One 4.5 L water-seal crock — the miso crock, currently under a weight bag.
- One 5 L food-grade bucket, retired 2026-09; it never sealed and attracted fruit flies.

## Temperature kit

- Three stick-on strip thermometers, stuck to the kombucha jar, the crock, and the starter jar.
- One seedling heat mat with a dial, set to 26 °C for kombucha runs between October and April.
- A cooler box plus two hot-water bottles for incubating koji rice around 32 °C.
- A small box dehydrator used for yogurt at 42 °C.

## Small stuff

- 0.1 g scale for salt and starter feedings; 1 g scale for bigger bakes.
- pH strips, 0–6 range, cut into thirds.
- Cotton cloth squares, elastic bands, a fine nylon mesh strainer for bottling.
- A shallow plastic tub with a lid that all second-stage bottles sit in — a direct consequence of the pomegranate incident, 2025-12-19.
- Grease pencil for labelling; masking tape peels off damp jars.

What I stopped buying: fancy airlock lids (a loose screw lid worked as well), pickle pebbles (a snapped-off jar lid wrapped in cloth does the same job).
