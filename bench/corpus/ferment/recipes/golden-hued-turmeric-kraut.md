# Golden-hued turmeric kraut

Written 2026-08-02. The fresh-rhizome twin of golden-kraut.md — painfully similar name, different jar: fresh turmeric and ginger instead of the ground powder, a hair more salt, and a slower, cooler ferment.

## Ingredients

- 1 kg green cabbage, shredded fine
- 21 g non-iodised salt (2.1%)
- 15 g fresh turmeric, grated fine (gloves, or accept orange hands for two days)
- 20 g fresh ginger, grated
- 1 tsp cracked black pepper

## Method

1. Toss cabbage and salt in a big bowl, wait 10 minutes until it starts to slump, then massage 5 minutes until a puddle forms.
2. Work the grated turmeric and ginger through with your hands until every shred is golden and the kitchen smells like a curry house in the best way.
3. Pack into the 1 L jar a fistful at a time, pressing each layer down until liquid rises over the cabbage. Leave 3 cm headspace.
4. Lid loose, 10 days at 19 °C — the cellar shelf, not the kitchen, is the point of this version. Then the fridge.

## Tasting notes

- Day 10: brighter and more floral than the pantry version, a slow warm hum from the fresh rhizome rather than the ground spice's earthiness.
- Week 3 in the fridge: the ginger comes forward and it reads almost like a pickle for cheese.
- Stains worse than golden kraut. Anything white within a metre is now yellow. This is the cost of doing business.

## Lessons

- Fresh turmeric is fibrous; grate it on the fine holes or you get threads through the jar.
- The extra 0.1% salt is deliberate: at 19 °C the batch runs slower, and the small bump keeps the surface quiet the whole ten days.
- Two jars side by side (this and golden kraut, August 2026) settle it: pantry version for grain bowls, this one for cheese boards.
