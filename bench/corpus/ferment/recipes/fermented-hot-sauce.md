# Fermented green-red hot sauce

Written 2026-08-23, the end-of-summer chilli-clearing sauce. Distinct from the house chili sauces (v1 and v2): this one is a brine ferment, green-forward, meant to sit in a shake bottle next to the stove.

## Ingredients

- 400 g mixed chilies: 60% green (jalapeño-type and green finger chilies), 40% ripe red for colour
- 6 cloves garlic, smashed
- 1 tsp cumin seed, lightly toasted
- 35 g non-iodised salt dissolved in 1 L water (3.5% brine) — you will not need all of it
- One 1.1 L tall jar, weights

## Method

1. Wipe, trim and halve the chilies. Pack upright into the jar with the garlic and cumin, then pour in brine until covered — mine took 800 ml.
2. Weights down, lid loose, 14 days at 21 °C. It turns olive-drab in the first week; that colour is the trade for the flavour and it comes back to life in the blender.
3. Strain, reserving the brine. Blend the solids with 40 ml of the brine to a pourable sauce — add more drop by drop; it should run off a spoon in 3 seconds.
4. Bottle (mine filled a 350 ml shake bottle), fridge. Keeps 6 months; flavour peaks around week 4.

## Tasting notes

- Day 14: green heat that lands at the back of the throat, garlicky, a cumin hum.
- Week 4 in the bottle: rounded, almost smoky, the best of the three sauces on fried eggs.
- Heat level: hotter than house chili sauce v2, sharper than v1.

## Lessons

- 3.5% is right for a 14-day run; my first attempt at 2% went soft and flat-tasting by day 10.
- Green chilies need the full 14 days; reds are ready at 10. Blend the batch when the greens say so.
- The reserved brine is a great dasher for soups — do not pour it away.
