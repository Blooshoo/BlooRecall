# Holding boxes through hidden stretches

2026-04-24 — Priya Chandrasekhar. Working note supporting occlusion protocol v2.

## The rule

When a subject slides out of view — a crossing, a pillar, a pan that leaves the
frame — we do not end the carrier. The box freezes at its last confirmed spot
and coasts on a straight line fitted from the previous nine positions, for up
to 45 frames. If the subject re-enters inside the coast corridor, we re-link
the label by predicted position alone. Looks play no part: the February rewrite
retired appearance matching after crowd crossings kept confusing carriers who
merely dressed alike.

## Last week's audit numbers

1,112 hidden stretches across 62 reels. 1,078 re-linked inside the corridor.
34 hit the 45-frame cap and were retired — a new carrier is born on re-entry
in those cases. That is 0.9% of carriers, inside the 1% budget we set at the
March review.

## Why the cap expiry dissolves instead of popping

At cap expiry the coasted box fades out over six frames instead of vanishing
between two frames. A popping box reads as a glitch in review sessions even
when the ledger is perfectly correct. Six frames is enough that the eye
forgives it; four is not; eight looks sluggish. Six.

## Open question

Diagonal crossings at high closing speed still force the cap about twice per
reel. A second-order coast (fitted velocity plus fitted acceleration) would
likely catch most of these; it costs one more column in the association matrix
and one more invariant to explain. Not scheduled; noted so it does not get
invented twice.
